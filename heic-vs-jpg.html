HEIC vs JPG: Which Is Better?

Your iPhone saves photos as HEIC, but most of the internet still runs on JPG. Which one should you use? This guide compares the two formats so you can decide.

Quick comparison

FeatureHEICJPG
File sizeSmaller, often around half at similar qualityLarger
Image qualityVery good, keeps more detail per megabyteGood, some quality loss from compression
Color depthSupports more than 8-bit color8-bit color

File size and storage

HEIC uses a more modern compression method, so a photo takes up much less space. If your phone is nearly full, keeping HEIC can save a lot of storage over thousands of pictures.

Image quality

At the same file size, HEIC usually looks better than JPG. At the same quality, HEIC files are smaller. For normal viewing on a phone or computer, most people will not notice a difference.

Compatibility

This is where JPG wins. You can open a JPG on nearly any phone, computer, website, printer and app. HEIC works well on Apple devices and on many newer systems, but it can fail on older software, some online forms and some printing services.

Editing

Most popular photo editors now support HEIC, but some tools still accept only JPG or PNG. If your workflow includes older software, JPG is the safe option.

Which should you choose?

  • Keep HEIC if you want to save space and you mainly use Apple devices.
  • Use JPG if you share photos with many people, upload to websites, or work on different devices and apps.
  • Do both: keep your originals in HEIC and convert copies to JPG only when you need them.
